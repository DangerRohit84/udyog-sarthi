# SIH Benchmark: Shortlisting + Winning Criteria for Software / Govt PS (SIH26130 lens)

**Date:** 2026-09-09
**Target PS:** SIH26130 — Govt. of Maharashtra, Dept. of Skills/Employment/Entrepreneurship/Innovation (via Maharashtra State Innovation Society) — "Efficiency in streamlining industrial approvals, compliance processes, and access to government support services" — Software / Miscellaneous / Smart Automation. 1/500 ideas submitted at time of check.
**Project:** Udyog Sarthi
**Method:** websearch + official SIH docs + winner post-mortems + MAITRI/NSWS live portals.

## 0. TL;DR — the weightage answer in 30 seconds

There is **no single official % table** published on sih.gov.in. There are **three different rubrics** in play and teams lose because they prepare for only one:

| Stage | What counts | Source |
|---|---|---|
| **A. Idea screening (national, PPT-only, 2–3 min per PPT)** | 8 params: **novelty, complexity, clarity & detail in prescribed format, feasibility, practicability, sustainability, scale of impact, user experience, future-work potential** | SIH 2025 Guidelines (MIC) |
| **B. College internal hackathon (steepest cut)** | Typically 100 marks, e.g. **Idea pitch 25 + Tech approach 30 + Feasibility 20 + Impact 15 + Innovation/Presentation/Q&A 10**. Tech approach is the single heaviest block. | GCELT SIH2026 notice; SIH2024 internal rubric |
| **C. Grand finale (36 hrs, 3 rounds weighted)** | **Round 1: 20%** (problem understanding, solution fit, innovation, tech feasibility, impact) → **Round 2: 30%** (prototype progress, technical soundness, usability, scalability, teamwork) → **Round 3: 50%** (functionality, performance, UX, market readiness, future scope). Separately, winner blogs summarise finale lens as **Innovation 20 / Tech 15 / MVP 15 / Impact 25 / Commercial viability 25**. | "Marking" doc (Scribd); Anish Prashun (SIH 2022 winner); Neurosignal guide (Problem 20 / Innovation 25 / Solution completeness 30 / Impact 25) |

**Synthesis for Udyog Sarthi:** at PPT stage, **clarity + novelty + feasibility** decide. At internal, **tech depth (30/100)** decides. At finale, **working demo + completeness** decide (Rounds 2+3 = 80% of final score). Half-built features count as **zero, not half** — strip scope to what you can demo confidently.

## 1. Official idea-selection criteria (what the national screeners tick)

From SIH 2025 Guidelines for College SPOCs (Ministry of Education's Innovation Cell):

> "Evaluation criteria will include **novelty of the idea, complexity, clarity and details in the prescribed format, feasibility, practicability, sustainability, scale of impact, user experience and potential for future work progression**."

Operational notes from the same document set:
- **6 slides max including title**, own template forbidden, PDF-only upload. Off-template = lost points before content is read.
- **500 ideas cap per PS.** Once a PS hits 500, it locks. Popular PSs close early — submit early, don't polish till deadline.
- **1 team → max 2 PSs.** One excellent submission beats two average ones.
- **4–5 teams per PS** may be invited to finale, but the PS owner **is not obliged to declare any winner** — SIH 2024 left SIH1529, SIH1564, SIH1692, SIH1697, SIH1776 with **no winner**. "Good enough for finale" ≠ "good enough for prize/money."
- Prize per PS: **Rs. 1,50,000**, only if the ministry/industry likes the winning idea. IP is split/shared with the PS owner.
- 2025 cap per college: **45 shortlisted + 5 waitlisted (max 50)**; 2024 was 25+5. Either way, if ~150 teams apply on campus and only ~40 actually submit, the internal round is still the steepest cut.

The 6-slide IDEA PPT skeleton (2024/2025/2026 identical):
1. Title + PS ID/title/theme/category/team
2. Proposed solution + how it addresses problem + **innovation/uniqueness**
3. Technical approach + stack + **flowchart/architecture/working prototype**
4. Feasibility/viability + risks + mitigation
5. Impact/benefits (social/economic/environmental)
6. Research + references/links

Evaluator spends **2–3 minutes** on your PPT (Reskilll). Walls of text = instant reject. Points/diagrams/infographics only.

## 2. Internal hackathon rubric — where most teams actually die

Two real rubrics found in the wild:

**a) SIH 2024 official internal rubric (Scribd "Rubrics for Internal Hackathon Evaluation")** — 4 blocks scored Not Acceptable(0) → Acceptable(3):
- Idea/solution (does it address the selected problem + innovative + clear?)
- Technical approach + methodology (presented? clear? improvable? clear implementation path?)
- Feasibility/viability (feasible? viable? risks identified? mitigation?)
- Impact/benefits (presented? minimal? huge + clear?)

**b) GCELT SIH2026 internal (100 marks, 5 faculty judges):**
- Idea pitch & problem understanding — **25**
- Technical approach — **30 (highest)**
- Feasibility & viability — **20**
- Impact & benefits — **15**
- Innovation + presentation + Q&A — **10**

Takeaway: faculty juries overweight **"can these 6 students actually build this?"** over pure novelty. "We'll use AI + blockchain + IoT" with no hour-by-hour plan scores worse than a narrower stack with a 12h/14h/10h build split (core → integration → testing) and something already running.

Internal disqualification traps (Zaid Sayyed internal playbook + college notices):
- Walls of text copied from Wikipedia/ChatGPT.
- Promising AI + blockchain + IoT together in 36 hours.
- 1 person talks, 5 silent (teamwork is scored at finale Round 2 explicitly).
- Arguing defensively when a professor raises a doubt.
- "Our app has better UI and is free" — faculty reject instantly. Correct frame: *theirs is a consumer product; ours is ministry-specific (compliance, offline-first, local language, official-data sync) which no private tool can do.*
- Wrong team shape: 6 members, ≥1 female, same institute, no inter-college teams; mentors 1–2 faculty. Missing female member = administrative reject.
- No prototype/screenshot/video. Even a seeded clickable demo beats theory.

## 3. Grand finale rubric — what "win" actually means

**3-round weighted model (Scribd "Marking" doc, widely circulated):**

- **Round 1 (20%): Solution feasibility & clarity** — problem understanding / solution appropriateness / innovation / technical feasibility / impact-sustainability (20 marks each = 100).
- **Round 2 (30%): Prototype development & progress** — prototype progress / technical soundness / usability-interface / scalability / teamwork (20 each = 100).
- **Round 3 (50%): Final demo & real-world impact** — functionality / performance / UX / market readiness / future scope (20 each = 100).
- Final = 0.2·R1 + 0.3·R2 + 0.5·R3.

**Winner-blog compression (Anish Prashun, SIH 2022 winner, SIH 2025 tips):**
- Innovation/novelty 20% · Technology 15% · MVP/demo 15% · Criticality/impact 25% · Commercial viability & cost-effectiveness 25%.

**2026 evaluator compression (Neurosignal, citing evaluator practice):**
- Problem understanding 20% · Innovation/novelty 25% · Solution design & completeness 30% · Impact & scalability 25%.

All three agree on the same hierarchy: **completeness + impact + viability outweigh raw novelty.** Anish's own confession: *"We won in 2022 despite not having time to create an entire prototype. We had a great team and we solved the core [problem]."* Judges reward the team that solves the core pain end-to-end over the team with 10 half-wired features.

Finale behaviours that separate winners (Dinesh/Team DORA SIH1370 Jharkhand infra; Zaid playbook; Neurosignal):
- **Demo first, 60 seconds, on the thing that works.** Slides only if asked. Everyone has a deck; few have something running.
- Show the **delta** on each evaluator visit (what broke last visit → what works now). Replaying the intro reads as "nothing moved."
- Name the **real system you'd integrate with** (REST / nightly CSV / API), the **pilot boundary** (one district, one depot, 50 users), the **day-to-day owner** (block officer, depot supervisor, district nodal officer), the **law that applies** (DPDP Act if personal data), **where data lives** (govt data usually can't leave India), and a **rough cost with the biggest line item** (inference/storage/SMS).
- Every member can explain their module **and the one next to it**. One person answering everything reads as one person did everything.
- Name **3 real failure modes** + handling (bad network, no GPS, garbage input). Claiming bulletproof in 36 hrs costs more than admitting flaws.
- Name your **weakest part honestly** when asked — it's a maturity test and usually the last question.

## 4. The novelty bar for SIH26130: MAITRI 2.0 + NSWS already exist

This is the single most dangerous fact for Udyog Sarthi. A generic "single-window dashboard with tracking" **already exists twice**:

**MAITRI 2.0 (Maharashtra, live):**
- Launched **4 Feb 2025 by CM Fadnavis**, now the **exclusive G2B channel** in Maharashtra (MAITRI 1.0 registrations closed).
- Single-window portal: project registration, common application form, departmental routing, online payment, **real-time tracking**, centralised document management.
- **Know Your Approvals / Wizard Module**: enter type/location/employees → get applicable permissions + forms + notifications.
- Legal backbone: **Maharashtra Industry, Trade & Investment Facilitation Act 2023** — time-bound disposal per RTS timelines, **auto-escalation of delayed applications to Empowered Committee**, joint inspections, deemed provisions.
- Grievance + handholding + EoDB coordination layer.

**NSWS (National Single Window System, nsws.gov.in, live):**
- **325 Central + ~2,200–2,400 State approvals** (counts vary by snapshot date; older articles cite 643 Central / 6,113 State — use current portal numbers with date-stamp), **32 Central ministries + 34 States/UTs**.
- **Know Your Approvals (KYA)** guided questionnaire, single sign-on, **central document repository (upload once, reuse)**, fee payment, tracking, renewals.
- Newer rails: **EntityLocker** (verified docs), **end-to-end API filing**, BharatAssist, dashboards/alerts to ministries.
- Explicit disclaimer: KYA is **guidance only**, final grant rests with the ministry/department.

**What this means:** if your PPT reads as "we'll build a portal where users apply and track," the screener writes *"already exists — see MAITRI/NSWS"* and moves on in 30 seconds. The PS itself knows this — read its "Expected Solution" closely. It does **not** ask for another portal. It asks for an **intelligent layer**: customised checklist generation, doc guidance + **pre-validation**, verified-data reuse, **parallel workflows**, inspection scheduling, **SLA tracking + alerts**, single dashboard for approvals/renewals/**incentives**, **regulatory knowledge engine, risk-based scrutiny, common inspection planning, grievance escalation, delay analytics**.

Your novelty must live in those adjectives, not in the noun "portal."

## 5. What winning gov-software SIH projects actually looked like (2023–2024)

Pattern, not one-offs. Every winner below bolted **one hard tech** onto a boring gov workflow and demoed it live:

- **HexxCode / Shiksha Niyojak (SIH1465, AICTE, Winner 2023):** unified curriculum portal with role-wise dashboards, collaborative editor with version control, discussion forum, LTP planner, visual analytics on feedback. Won because it modelled **multi-stakeholder workflow + versioning**, not just "a website."
- **Team Bharat / Institute Verification Portal (SIH2023 finals):** inter-state scholarship verification — Aadhaar auth, **blockchain (IPFS/NSID) for tamper-proof docs, PaddleOCR/EasyOCR template verification**, computer-vision data extraction, chatbot for queries. Lesson: **trust tech (OCR + hash) on top of CRUD** is what made a "portal" win.
- **Team Avengers / News 360 feedback (PS1329, 2023):** crawled 12k+ articles (BeautifulSoup/Selenium), **DistilBERT classification (83%) + RoBERTa sentiment**, email alerts on negative news, Next.js + Django. Lesson: **a measured model (83%, named) beats "we'll use AI."**
- **Team DORA / Jharkhand infra monitoring (SIH1370, Winner 2023):** **AI image diff (CCTV day-over-day) + material-consumption estimate + GIS (Leaflet) + project/task system + React Native field app.** Round 2 was pure integration pressure (ML↔app↔GIS); Round 3 app broke 1 hr before judging — they reverted via WhatsApp-stored code, all members pitched their slice, BMC for rollout. Lesson: **integration + everyone speaks + business model slide** = brownie points that offset a crash.
- **Cryptonite / PhishTrap (NTRO, Winner 2023):** 4-module real-time anti-phishing extension+app. Lesson: narrow, deep, real-time.
- **Negative signal:** 5 PSs in SIH2024 got **no winner at all**. Deployability threshold is real — "interesting demo" without a pilot/owner/cost story leaves prize money unawarded.

Common winner stack traits: familiar stack (≥50–60% team familiarity — React/Node/Express/Mongo or Django/Flask + Supabase/Postgres), one ML/NLP/CV or GIS piece with **named model + accuracy + failure handling**, seeded real data (data.gov.in / ministry reports / Kaggle-nearby), backup video on two devices, PDF on pen drive + email.

## 6. Common rejection reasons (college/internal + national screening)

**College/internal (from Zaid, GCELT, EWIT prep guide, Reskilll):**
1. Choosing a PS that doesn't match team skills; can't name who owns the hardest part.
2. Textbook pitch: reads the PS aloud instead of naming **one real persona + what they do today + what it costs** (time/money/errors).
3. No architecture/workflow diagram; "we'll use AI" with no HOW (which model, which data, what accuracy, what fallback).
4. No prototype — not even seeded screenshots. Dashboard with zero rows looks unfinished even if code is perfect.
5. Overpromising everything in the PS; no out-of-scope boundary. Teams that draw a boundary look like they've planned.
6. Can't answer domain questions (they're about the ecosystem, not your code): current process, blocker (connectivity/incentive/data ownership), scaling (who onboards next district, what training, what breaks in new language/terrain), cost owner (which dept/scheme budget).
7. Team dysfunction signals: one speaker, talking over each other, no role split (frontend/backend/ML-data/design/research/pitch/demo-operator).

**National screening PPT (from Anish + SIH instructions + evaluator interviews):**
1. **Off-topic drift** — doesn't address every word of the PS. Non-negotiable; great solution to wrong problem = reject.
2. **Off-template / paragraphs / >6 slides / wrong format** (not PDF).
3. **Generic AI slop** — judges spot recycled/AI-generated content instantly. Original + specific > perfect grammar.
4. **No technical depth** — buzzword stack (AI+blockchain+IoT) with no data source, no integration path, no hour plan.
5. **No proof** — no prototype screenshot/video/link, no references, no data source named (say data.gov.in / ministry report / Bhuvan for geo / "realistic schema modelled on official form" where access is restricted).
6. **"Better UI and free"** as differentiation vs MAITRI/NSWS. Zero marks.
7. **Undefended numbers** — "99% accuracy," "50% faster" with no test set/sample size/failure costs (false positive vs false negative + human check/threshold/fallback).
8. Copy of a previous SIH solution; duplicate/idea already featured elsewhere (ineligible).
9. Track/PS misalignment; hitting the 500-cap too late; admin errors (team size ≠6, no female member, institute name in team name, authorisation letter wrong).

## 7. SIH26130: shortlist + win vs "just another CRUD dashboard"

The PS text (SIH2026.vuce.in mirror of sih.gov.in) in one line: **both sides are in pain** — applicants (which approvals? which docs? timelines? queries? incentives?) and departments (incomplete apps, repetitive scrutiny, manual coordination, no bottleneck visibility, inconsistent compliance).

Expected outcomes named: reduced approval time, fewer incomplete applications, transparency, lower compliance cost, better scheme utilisation, stronger EoDB.

**Shortlist threshold (PPT must show all five, concretely):**
1. **Customised checklist engine** — inputs (sector/location/size/stage) → applicable approvals with statute/timeline/fee/docs. Name the rule source (MAITRI KYA list + 2023 Act schedules) and how rules update without code change (versioned knowledge base).
2. **Doc pre-validation before submission** — checklist per approval, format/size checks, OCR field extraction + cross-form consistency (name/address/PAN drift), "completeness score" that predicts department return. This directly attacks "incomplete applications."
3. **SLA brain** — per-approval RTS clock, query-stop-the-clock logic, breach-risk prediction, auto-escalation draft per the 2023 Act's Empowered Committee path. A countdown bar is CRUD; a **breach predictor + escalation packet** is novelty.
4. **Parallel-workflow + inspection planner** — dependency graph (what can run parallel vs sequential), common/joint inspection slot proposal (the Act wants joint inspections), renewal calendar.
5. **Dept-side bottleneck analytics** — where files pile up, median days by dept/approval, return reasons ranked, incentive/scheme matcher (eligibility from profile, not a links page).

**Win threshold (finale demo must prove three):**
- **One AI moment that works live:** e.g. upload a real MAITRI-form-like PDF → OCR extract → completeness + inconsistency flags in <30s, with confidence + human-review queue. Quote accuracy on *your* test set (n=…) and where it fails.
- **One workflow moment that MAITRI doesn't do well today:** parallel-path visualiser + deemed-escalation timer counting down per the Act, or grievance-to-escalation packet generator.
- **One numbers moment:** "cuts reporting/tracking delay from X weeks to Y days" / "catches Z% of returns pre-submission on test set" — one memorable figure, not five.

Explicitly out-of-scope (say so in PPT — maturity signal): actual grant of approvals (statutory power stays with Competent Authority), payments, DigiLocker/EntityLocker deep integration (show as "integration path: REST/nightly CSV," not "done").

Language/inclusion edge for Maharashtra PS: Marathi + English, low-bandwidth/assisted mode (shared-device/CSC-operator flow), offline-draft-then-sync. Most teams skip this; for a state-ministry PS it's scored under practicability/UX.

## 8. Seven harsh truths this team must hear

1. **MAITRI 2.0 + NSWS already do 80% of your first draft.** If your PPT's core verb is "apply and track," you've lost. Your product is the *intelligence around* the portal (pre-validation, SLA prediction, bottleneck analytics, scheme matching) — or nothing.
2. **Nobody will believe "AI checklist" without a rule source and a test.** Name the statutes/forms/timelines you encoded, how many approvals you covered, versioning strategy, and your accuracy on n samples. "99% AI" with no denominator is read as lying.
3. **A dashboard with fake-looking lorem rows insults a ministry evaluator.** Seed with realistic Maharashtra-shaped data (MIDC zones, real approval names/fees/timelines from MAITRI KYA pages), date-stamp the snapshot, and label synthetic rows as synthetic-modelled-on-official-schema.
4. **Tech approach is 30/100 internally and Rounds 2+3 are 80% of the finale — your PPT's weakest slide is the most weighted one.** One flowchart + stack-justification + hour plan + integration path beats three slides of "features."
5. **One speaker = one-person project in the jury's eyes.** Teamwork is literally scored. Every member must own a module and its neighbour, and Q&A must be pre-split (domain / build / impact). Two people answering one question makes the first answer sound incomplete.
6. **Half-built features score zero.** The team that demos 3 flows that survive questioning beats the team with 10 menu items where 4 crash. Decide in advance which 3 the jury must see (problem → hardest demo → impact number) in case you're stopped at minute 4.
7. **No winner is a real outcome.** Five PSs went unwon in SIH2024. The ministry would rather keep Rs. 1.5L than fund an undeployable demo. "Pilot in one district, owned by [named role], paid by [named scheme], data stays in India, DPDP-handled" is what converts shortlist → winner.
